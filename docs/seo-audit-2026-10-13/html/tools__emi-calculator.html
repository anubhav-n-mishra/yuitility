How does the EMI Calculation work?

An EMI is calculated based on the reducing balance method. The formula applied is:
EMI = [P x R x (1+R)^N] / [(1+R)^N - 1]Where:
• P: Principal Loan Amount.
• R: Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100).
• N: Number of Monthly Installments.
This calculator assumes the interest rate remains constant throughout the tenure. Check your amortization schedule to see how more of your payments go toward principal as the loan matures.

The maths behind the emi calculator

So you can check the result rather than take it on trust.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1)

  • ·P — principal, the amount actually borrowed (after down payment, before fees)
  • ·r — interest rate per month = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
  • ·n — tenure in months

Worked example

A ₹50,00,000 home loan at 8.5% for 20 years.

r (monthly rate)
8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0070833
n (months)
20 × 12 = 240
(1 + r)ⁿ
1.0070833²⁴⁰ = 5.44127
EMI
5000000 × 0.0070833 × 5.44127 ÷ 4.44127

EMI = ₹43,391 per month. Total repaid ₹1,04,13,879, of which ₹54,13,879 is interest — more than the amount borrowed.

In month 1 of that loan, ₹35,417 of the ₹43,391 payment is interest and only ₹7,975 reduces the principal — 18% of your payment. That ratio is why prepaying early is worth far more than prepaying late.

Where this calculation usually goes wrong

Comparing EMIs instead of total interest

A longer tenure always produces a smaller, more attractive EMI. On the ₹8,00,000 car loan at 9.5% above, stretching 60 months to 84 months drops the EMI from ₹16,801 to ₹13,075 — and raises total interest from ₹2,08,089 to ₹2,98,316. You pay ₹90,000 for the comfort of a smaller number.

Using the sanctioned amount as the principal

Processing fees, insurance bundled into the loan and GST on those fees are often deducted from disbursal but charged interest on. Use the amount credited plus anything financed, not the headline sanction.

Assuming the rate is fixed

Most floating-rate loans reprice against a benchmark. When the rate moves, lenders usually hold the EMI constant and change the tenure instead, so the loan quietly gets longer rather than more expensive per month.

Edge cases worth knowing

Zero interest rate

The standard formula divides by ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), which is zero when r = 0. At 0% the EMI is simply P ÷ n. This calculator handles that case rather than returning an error or infinity.

Part-prepayment

A lump-sum prepayment reduces principal immediately. You then choose to keep the EMI (tenure shortens, maximum interest saved) or reduce the EMI (tenure unchanged, less saved). Keeping the EMI is almost always the cheaper option.

Frequently Asked Questions about the EMI Calculator

Frequently asked questions about calculation formulas, privacy, and usage.

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = [P x R x (1+R)^N] / [(1+R)^N - 1], where P is the principal, R is the monthly interest rate, and N is the number of monthly installments.

Why does more interest get paid in the early months?

In a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal. Early on, the outstanding balance is highest, so a larger share of each EMI goes toward interest rather than principal.

Does prepayment reduce my EMI or my tenure?

Depends on the lender's policy - some reduce your tenure while keeping EMI the same, others reduce your EMI while keeping tenure the same. Check with your lender which option applies.

Is a lower EMI always better?

Not necessarily - a lower EMI usually means a longer tenure, which increases total interest paid over the life of the loan.
